Lynx Visual Division — Client Outreach System
Purpose

This file defines the client outreach system for Lynx Visual Division.

It helps find, evaluate, contact, and convert potential clients for:

brand identity
logo redesign
landing pages
business websites
interactive catalogs
creator portfolios
website refreshes
audits
maintenance plans
lightweight web apps
AI-assisted workflow consulting

The goal is not to spam businesses.

The goal is to identify real opportunities where better design, clearer UX, stronger identity, and better web implementation can help a business present itself better and generate more trust.

Core Outreach Principle

Outreach should feel useful, respectful, and specific.

The message should communicate:

“I noticed something real, and I may be able to help.”

Not:

“Your website is bad. Buy from me.”

Lynx Visual Division should approach potential clients with:

respect
clarity
useful observation
short messages
no pressure
no fake urgency
no exaggerated promises
no insults
no spam behavior

Prospect Qualification

Before contacting a prospect, score them.

Rate each from 1 to 5.

Business Fit

Does this prospect match Lynx services?

1 = poor fit
5 = excellent fit

Website Opportunity

Is there a clear web/design opportunity?

1 = no clear opportunity
5 = strong opportunity

Ability to Pay

Does the business seem able to invest?

1 = unlikely
5 = likely

Contact Accessibility

Is there a clear way to reach them?

1 = hard to contact
5 = easy to contact

Portfolio Value

Would this project improve the Lynx portfolio?

1 = low value
5 = strong value

Risk Level

Is the project safe and manageable?

1 = high risk
5 = low risk

Priority Score

Add:

Business Fit
Website Opportunity
Ability to Pay
Contact Accessibility
Portfolio Value
Risk Level

Score interpretation:

25–30 = high priority

Outreach Strategy
Step 1 — Observe

Find one positive observation.

Examples:

strong reviews
good product photography
active social media
clear niche
interesting service
strong local presence
nice visual concept
good community
Step 2 — Identify One Opportunity

Pick only one or two issues.

Do not overwhelm.

Examples:

mobile contact flow could be clearer
website does not show services quickly
catalog is hard to browse
logo does not work well as favicon
Instagram looks stronger than the website
CTA is hard to find
site could better reflect the quality of the business
Step 3 — Offer Small Value

Offer:

short visual audit
3–5 practical improvements
homepage review
mobile UX review
brand/logo digital review
landing page idea
catalog idea
Step 4 — Invite, Do Not Pressure

Use low-pressure language.

Examples:

“If useful, I can send…”
“I noticed one opportunity…”
“No pressure at all…”
“I thought this could be useful…”
“Happy to share a few practical ideas…”

Response Handling
If They Say Yes

Reply:

Great, thank you. I’ll prepare a short audit focused on the most practical improvements: mobile experience, clarity, visual trust, contact flow, and SEO basics.

Then prepare:

short audit
top 3–5 issues
top 3–5 opportunities
recommended service
soft next step
If They Ask Price Immediately

Reply:

The price depends on the scope. A focused landing page, a visual refresh, a full website redesign, and an interactive catalog are all different levels of work.

The best first step would be a short audit so I can recommend the most practical option instead of guessing.

If They Ask “What Do You Do?”

Reply:

I help small businesses, creators, and growing brands improve their digital presence through visual identity, logo refinement, landing pages, websites, interactive catalogs, and lightweight web apps.

The goal is to make the brand look more professional, communicate more clearly, and make it easier for visitors to take action.

If They Are Not Interested

Reply:

No problem at all. Thank you for your time. I’ll keep following your work, and if later you want to improve the website, identity, or create a specific landing page/catalog, I’d be happy to help.

audit notes

Discovery Questions

Use when a prospect is interested.

Ask:

What is the main goal of the website or digital presence right now?
What do most clients ask before buying or contacting you?
Where do most leads or sales come from today?
Do you want more calls, WhatsApp messages, bookings, purchases, or quote requests?
Do you already have logo files, photos, copy, and brand assets?
Do you need a simple refresh or a full redesign?
Do you update your website/products often?
Would a monthly maintenance plan be useful?
Is the priority visual trust, better contact flow, SEO, catalog, or conversion?
Is there a deadline or campaign date?

Weekly Outreach Routine

Use a simple routine.

Weekly Goal

Start with:

5 to 10 reviewed prospects per week
3 to 5 high-quality outreach messages per week
1 follow-up batch per week

Do not start with mass outreach.

Quality first.

Weekly Steps
Find prospects
Score prospects
Audit top prospects lightly
Send personalized messages
Track responses
Follow up once
Prepare audits for interested leads
Convert responses into discovery/proposal
