Workflow — Client Website Audit
Purpose

This workflow helps Lynx Visual Division review existing business websites and identify opportunities for redesign, UX improvement, SEO improvement, conversion improvement, maintenance plans, or new landing pages.

Use this workflow when prospecting potential clients from:

Google Maps
Instagram
LinkedIn
local directories
referrals
competitor lists
businesses with outdated websites
businesses with weak mobile experience
creators with no professional website
brands relying only on social media

The goal is not to criticize the business.

The goal is to identify useful opportunities and start a respectful conversation.

A good audit should be:

specific
respectful
useful
honest
visually clear
business-oriented
not exaggerated
not insulting
not spammy

Phase 1 — Prospect Selection
Goal

Decide if a business is worth auditing.

Good Prospect Signals

Look for businesses that have:

active Google Maps profile
real reviews
real product/service
outdated website
weak mobile experience
strong business but weak digital presence
poor contact flow
no clear CTA
unclear services
bad product presentation
old visual style
no landing page for a specific offer
good Instagram but weak website
business with visible growth potential
Weak Prospect Signals

Avoid or deprioritize businesses that:

have no clear contact info
seem inactive
have no reviews or digital presence
already have a strong modern website
are too large for your current capacity
require complex legal, medical, financial, payroll, or regulated systems
need advanced backend systems beyond your current scope
appear to be a poor fit culturally or commercially

Cold Email Structure

Subject:

Quick idea for [Business Name]’s website

Body:

Hi [Name/Business Team],

I came across [Business Name] while looking at [industry/location] and noticed [specific positive observation].

I also noticed one small opportunity: [specific website observation], especially for users visiting from mobile.

A clearer [landing page/contact flow/catalog/service page] could help new visitors understand [service/product] faster and make it easier to [contact/book/buy/request info].

I work with small and growing brands on premium websites, landing pages, catalogs, and web experiences.

If useful, I can send you a short visual audit with 3–5 practical improvements for your current website.

Best,
[Name]
Lynx Visual Division

The first message should not try to sell everything. It should open a door.

Phase 11 — Response Handling
Goal

Know what to do if the prospect replies.

If They Say Yes

Send:

Thanks. I’ll prepare a short audit focused on the most useful improvements: mobile experience, clarity, contact flow, visual trust, and SEO basics.

Then prepare:

3–5 issue audit
3–5 opportunity audit
suggested package
next step offer
If They Ask Price

Respond with ranges, not a final price, unless scope is clear.

Suggested structure:

The price depends on the scope. For example, a focused landing page or website refresh is usually different from a full multi-page redesign or catalog. If you want, I can first send a short audit and then suggest the most practical option.

If They Are Not Interested

Respond politely:

No problem at all. I appreciate your time. I’ll keep following your work, and if later you want to improve the website or create a specific landing page, I’d be happy to help.

If They Do Not Reply

Follow up once after a few days.

Do not spam.

Follow-Up Message

Hi [Name], just following up on the website note I sent. No pressure at all — I simply thought there were a few practical improvements that could make the mobile/contact experience clearer for new visitors. Happy to send the short audit if useful.

Quality Rule

The goal is to build reputation, not pressure people.

Discovery Questions

Ask:

Phase 15 — Ethics and Tone
Goal

Keep outreach professional and respectful.

Rules

Do:

be specific
be useful
be respectful
mention positives
offer a small next step
be honest about scope
avoid fake guarantees
avoid fear-based selling

Do not:

insult the website
shame the business
exaggerate problems
promise rankings
promise revenue
send huge unsolicited reports
pressure the prospect
pretend to be a customer
use manipulative urgency
overstep regulated industries
Final Rule

The audit should feel like:

“I noticed something useful and I can help.”

Not:

“Your website is bad and you need me.”

Final Client Audit Checklist

Before contacting a prospect, confirm:

 business is active
 website exists
 one positive observation identified
 one real opportunity identified
 contact channel found
 outreach message is short
 message is respectful
 no fake claims
 no aggressive selling
 suggested offer is realistic
 prospect priority is noted
 Final Rule

A client website audit is not about proving that the client is wrong.

It is about showing that better design, clearer UX, stronger SEO foundations, and a cleaner conversion path can help the business present itself better and receive better opportunities.
